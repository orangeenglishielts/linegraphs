Section 2: Plan Before Writing
Two minutes of planning saves ten minutes of rewriting
The line graph shows average commute times (minutes) in three cities between 2000 and 2020.
Overview: Overall, commute times increased in all three cities, though one rose far more sharply than the others.

Grouping: By trend — group the city with the sharp rise separately from the two with gradual change.

Body 1 figures: City with sharp rise: e.g. 20 → 45 minutes.
Body 2 figures: Other two cities: e.g. 25 → 32 minutes, and 30 → 35 minutes.
Two minutes here saves you from rewriting the whole thing later!
Section 3: A Full Example, Annotated
One graph, one complete response — see exactly why each part works
The graph below shows the number of visitors to three London museums between 1990 and 2020.
Watch the numbers: notice every figure below is “2 million”, “6 million”, “3 million” — never “millions”. Million, thousand, and hundred stay singular whenever a specific number comes right before them.
Introduction
“The line graph illustrates how many people visited three London museums over the 30-year period from 1990 to 2020.”
Paraphrases the prompt — new structure, same categories.
Overview
“Overall, one museum consistently attracted far more visitors than the other two, while numbers at all three venues rose considerably.”
Two general trends. No figures at all.
Body 1
“The British Museum began at around 2 million in 1990 and climbed steadily to roughly 6 million by 2020, remaining the most popular throughout.”
Varied verb (‘climbed’) + specific figures + superlative (‘most popular’).
Body 2
“In contrast, the other two began under 1 million. While the Tate grew gradually to 3 million, the Science Museum fluctuated before reaching 2.5 million.”
Two linking words (‘in contrast’, ‘while’) + comparative figures + varied verbs (‘grew’, ‘fluctuated’).
See those linking words? That's the difference between a list and a comparison.
