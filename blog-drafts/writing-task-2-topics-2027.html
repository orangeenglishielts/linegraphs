What IELTS Writing Task 2 topics should you prepare for in 2027?

Nobody can reliably predict which essay questions will appear in your exam. What you can do is prepare for a wide range of themes and build the skills to respond to any question well.

As a former IELTS examiner for Writing, I'd recommend focusing on developing your ideas, explaining your arguments clearly and answering every part of the question, rather than memorising model essays.

In this guide you'll find 15 useful topics to practise. Each one has:

  • an exam-style essay question, with the question type labelled
  • ideas to consider for your body paragraphs
  • five useful phrases, with meanings and examples
  • one quick activity to practise a key essay skill

The answers are at the end of the post.

Before you start: a simple essay structure

You'll get more from the questions below if you plan each essay with the same clear structure.

Introduction (four parts)

  1. A general background statement about the topic
  2. A paraphrase of the question
  3. Your position (a clear answer to the question)
  4. An outline that names the two ideas coming up in your body paragraphs

The outline is useful because it forces you to plan before you write.

Example (topic 4, remote working):

In recent years, technology has changed the way many people work. As a result, a growing number of employees now work from home rather than travelling to an office each day. In my view, the benefits of this trend outweigh the drawbacks. This essay will discuss how remote working saves commuting time and how it can improve work-life balance.

Body paragraphs

Write two balanced body paragraphs, each with one main idea that you explain and support. In problem and solution essays, try pairing each problem with its solution: Body 1 = problem 1 + its solution, Body 2 = problem 2 + its solution. This is usually more cohesive than listing all the problems and then all the solutions.

Conclusion

Sum up your main ideas and restate your position. Don't add new ideas.

1. Artificial intelligence and employment

Question type: discuss both views and give your opinion

Practice question

Some people believe that artificial intelligence will improve working conditions, while others think it will lead to widespread unemployment.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Ideas to consider

  • Automation can reduce repetitive and physically demanding tasks.
  • AI may create employment opportunities in emerging industries.
  • Workers whose jobs are automated may struggle to find alternative employment.
  • Employers and governments may need to provide retraining opportunities.

Useful vocabulary

  • the workforce: all the people who work in a country or company. AI is likely to change the skills the workforce needs.
  • automation: machines or software doing work people used to do. Automation has already transformed manufacturing.
  • repetitive tasks: the same simple actions done again and again. AI can take over repetitive tasks such as data entry.
  • technological advancement: progress in technology. Technological advancement has always created new types of jobs.
  • reskilling: learning new skills for a different job. Governments could fund reskilling programmes for older workers.

Quick practice: paraphrase the question

Rewrite this part of the question in your own words, without copying more than two or three words:

"Some people believe that artificial intelligence will improve working conditions."

Hint: you can change the verb (believe), the noun phrase (working conditions) or the sentence structure.

2. Education and practical skills

Question type: opinion (to what extent do you agree or disagree?)

Practice question

Some people believe that schools should spend more time teaching practical life skills, such as managing money and preparing meals, rather than traditional academic subjects.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Ideas to consider

  • Practical skills help young people become independent.
  • Academic subjects provide important foundations for further education.
  • Not all children have opportunities to learn life skills at home.
  • Schools could integrate practical skills into existing subjects.

Useful vocabulary

  • financial literacy: understanding how to manage money. Financial literacy helps young adults avoid debt.
  • academic achievement: success in school subjects and exams. Many schools are judged mainly on academic achievement.
  • independent living: being able to look after yourself. Cooking is an essential skill for independent living.
  • transferable skills: skills that are useful in many situations. Teamwork is a transferable skill that every student needs.
  • the school curriculum: the subjects and content taught at school. Budgeting could be added to the school curriculum.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: write a clear position

This position sentence is weak because it doesn't answer the question:

"This essay will discuss practical skills and academic subjects."

Write a stronger version that clearly says how far you agree. Hint: use a phrase such as I largely agree, I partly agree or I disagree, and give a short reason.

3. Climate change and individual responsibility

Question type: opinion

Practice question

Some people believe that individuals can do little to improve the environment and that only governments and large companies can make a significant difference.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Ideas to consider

  • Governments can introduce environmental regulations.
  • Companies can change manufacturing and distribution practices.
  • Individual consumer choices can influence market demand.
  • Collective changes in behaviour may have a considerable impact.

Useful vocabulary

  • environmental impact: the effect something has on the environment. Fast fashion has a huge environmental impact.
  • collective action: people acting together towards the same goal. Collective action can push companies to change.
  • carbon emissions: CO2 released into the air. Transport is a major source of carbon emissions.
  • government regulation: rules made and enforced by the government. Stricter government regulation could reduce pollution.
  • sustainable consumption: buying and using things in a way that protects the planet. Sustainable consumption means buying less and choosing carefully.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: avoid overgeneralising

Strong essays avoid claims that are too absolute. Rewrite these two sentences so they are more careful and accurate:

  1. Individuals cannot do anything about climate change.
  2. All companies only care about profit.

Hint: try words like alone, many, often, tend to or are unlikely to.

4. Remote working

Question type: advantages outweigh disadvantages?

Practice question

An increasing number of people are able to work from home rather than travelling to a workplace every day.

Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?

Ideas to consider

  • Employees may save time and money on commuting.
  • Flexible working can improve work-life balance.
  • Some employees experience isolation and communication difficulties.
  • Working from home is not suitable for every profession.

Useful vocabulary

  • remote working: working away from the office, usually at home. Remote working became common after 2020.
  • productivity: how much work someone gets done. Some studies link fewer interruptions with higher productivity.
  • commuting costs: money spent travelling to and from work. Remote workers save on commuting costs.
  • professional isolation: feeling cut off from colleagues. Professional isolation can affect motivation.
  • flexible arrangements: agreements that allow changes to where or when people work. Flexible arrangements suit many parents.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: which answer is clearer?

In an "outweigh" essay, the reader needs to know which side is stronger. Which position sentence works better, and why?

A: "Working from home has both advantages and disadvantages."

B: "Although remote working can leave some employees feeling isolated, I believe the time and money it saves make the advantages more significant."

5. Social media and young people

Question type: problem and solution

Practice question

Many young people spend a considerable amount of time using social media.

What problems can this cause, and what measures could be taken to address them?

Ideas to consider

  • Excessive screen time may interfere with sleep and studying.
  • Social comparison may negatively affect self-esteem.
  • Social media can expose young people to misleading information.
  • Digital literacy education and appropriate parental guidance may help.

Useful vocabulary

  • excessive screen time: too much time looking at screens. Excessive screen time late at night can disturb sleep.
  • self-esteem: how much you value yourself. Comparing yourself with influencers can damage self-esteem.
  • online safety: protecting yourself from risks on the internet. Schools now teach online safety from a young age.
  • digital literacy: the ability to use and judge online information. Digital literacy helps students spot false news.
  • parental supervision: parents checking what their children do. Younger children need parental supervision online.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: pair each problem with a solution

Remember: Body 1 = problem 1 + its solution, Body 2 = problem 2 + its solution. Match each problem to the most suitable solution.

  1. Late-night scrolling affects sleep and schoolwork.
  2. Teenagers compare themselves with edited images.
  3. Young people believe misleading posts.

a) Teach students how to check sources in digital literacy lessons. b) Encourage families to keep phones out of bedrooms at night. c) Show students how images are filtered and edited, and discuss realistic expectations.

6. Healthcare and healthy lifestyles

Question type: opinion

Practice question

Some people believe that governments should spend more money preventing illnesses than treating people who are already ill.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Ideas to consider

  • Preventive healthcare may reduce long-term healthcare costs.
  • Public health campaigns can encourage healthier decisions.
  • Not all illnesses can be prevented.
  • Healthcare systems must continue to provide effective treatment.

Useful vocabulary

  • preventive healthcare: care that stops illness before it starts. Vaccinations are a key part of preventive healthcare.
  • public health: the health of the population as a whole. Smoking bans have improved public health.
  • chronic illness: a long-lasting health condition. Chronic illnesses such as diabetes are expensive to treat.
  • medical intervention: treatment given by doctors. Some conditions always require medical intervention.
  • healthcare expenditure: money spent on healthcare. Healthcare expenditure rises as populations age.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: word forms

Complete each sentence with the correct form of prevent: prevention, preventive, preventable

  1. Many heart problems are ______ with a healthier diet.
  2. ______ is often cheaper than cure.
  3. Governments could invest more in ______ measures such as health checks.

7. Housing and urban development

Question type: causes and solutions (two questions)

Practice question

In many large cities, the cost of housing has increased considerably, making it difficult for young people to afford a home.

What are the causes of this problem, and what solutions can you suggest?

Ideas to consider

  • Population growth can increase demand for housing.
  • Construction may not keep pace with demand.
  • Governments could encourage the development of affordable housing.
  • Better transport links could connect people to less expensive areas.

Useful vocabulary

  • a housing shortage: not enough homes for the people who need them. Many capital cities face a serious housing shortage.
  • affordable accommodation: housing that people on average incomes can pay for. Students struggle to find affordable accommodation.
  • urban development: building and planning in cities. Urban development should include green spaces.
  • population density: how many people live in an area. High population density pushes up rents.
  • the property market: the buying and selling of homes. Investors have changed the property market in many cities.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: answer both questions

A common reason for a lower Task Response score is answering only part of the question. For this question, write down:

  1. the two things you must do
  2. one cause and one matching solution you could use in Body 1

Hint: the solution should respond directly to the cause.

8. Tourism and local communities

Question type: advantages outweigh disadvantages?

Practice question

International tourism brings economic benefits to many countries but can also create problems for local communities.

Do the advantages of international tourism outweigh its disadvantages?

Ideas to consider

  • Tourism creates employment and supports local businesses.
  • Tourism revenue can help preserve cultural attractions.
  • Overcrowding can reduce residents' quality of life.
  • Tourism can increase pressure on housing and natural resources.

Useful vocabulary

  • tourism revenue: money earned from tourists. Tourism revenue funds the upkeep of historic sites.
  • cultural heritage: a place's traditions, buildings and history. Visitors help protect cultural heritage through ticket sales.
  • overcrowding: too many people in one place. Overcrowding makes daily life difficult for residents.
  • local infrastructure: roads, transport and services in an area. Tourism puts pressure on local infrastructure.
  • sustainable tourism: tourism that doesn't damage places or communities. Sustainable tourism limits visitor numbers at fragile sites.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: choose the best topic sentence

Which sentence is the best start for a body paragraph about economic benefits?

A: "Tourism is a very big industry in the world nowadays."

B: "The main advantage of international tourism is the income and jobs it brings to local communities."

C: "Many tourists like to visit famous places such as Paris and Rome."

9. Online learning

Question type: discuss both views and give your opinion

Practice question

Some people believe that online learning will eventually replace traditional classroom education, while others think that face-to-face teaching will always be necessary.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Ideas to consider

  • Online courses can make education more accessible.
  • Learners may benefit from flexible study schedules.
  • Face-to-face interaction can support collaboration and motivation.
  • Practical subjects may require specialist equipment or facilities.

Useful vocabulary

  • accessibility: how easy something is to reach or use. Online courses improve accessibility for people in rural areas.
  • independent learning: studying without a teacher guiding every step. Online study requires strong independent learning skills.
  • learner engagement: how involved and interested students are. Group work can increase learner engagement.
  • face-to-face interaction: meeting and talking in person. Some skills develop best through face-to-face interaction.
  • blended learning: a mix of online and classroom study. Many universities now use blended learning.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: link your paragraphs

Good essays connect paragraphs, not just sentences. One way is a topic sentence that links back to the previous paragraph.

Body 1 argued that online learning makes education more accessible. Complete this opening for Body 2:

"However, despite ______, ______."

10. Consumerism and advertising

Question type: opinion

Practice question

Advertising encourages people to buy products that they do not really need.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Ideas to consider

  • Advertising can encourage impulse purchases.
  • Social media influencers may create unrealistic expectations.
  • Advertising also informs consumers about useful products.
  • Financial education could encourage more responsible spending.

Useful vocabulary

  • consumer behaviour: how and why people buy things. Social media has changed consumer behaviour.
  • disposable income: money left after paying for essentials. Young professionals often have more disposable income.
  • impulse purchases: things bought suddenly without planning. Online "one-click" buttons encourage impulse purchases.
  • persuasive techniques: methods used to convince people. Adverts use persuasive techniques such as limited-time offers.
  • material possessions: things people own. Advertising links happiness with material possessions.

Quick practice: develop the idea

Complete this chain to turn one short point into a developed argument: main idea → explanation → example → result.

  1. Main idea: Advertising often creates a sense of urgency.
  2. Explanation: ______
  3. Example: ______
  4. Result: ______

11. Ageing populations

Question type: problem and solution

Practice question

In many countries, people are living longer than they did in the past.

What challenges does this create for society, and how can these challenges be addressed?

Ideas to consider

  • An ageing population can increase demand for healthcare services.
  • Pension systems may face additional financial pressure.
  • Older people can contribute valuable experience to society.
  • Flexible retirement arrangements may help people remain economically active.

Useful vocabulary

  • life expectancy: how long people are expected to live. Life expectancy has risen in most countries.
  • an ageing population: a population with a growing share of older people. Japan has one of the world's oldest populations.
  • the pension system: how money is provided to retired people. The pension system is under pressure as fewer people work.
  • healthcare provision: the healthcare services available. Healthcare provision must expand to meet demand.
  • the retirement age: the age at which people stop working. Some governments have raised the retirement age.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: fix the mistakes

Each sentence has one error. Find and correct it.

  1. The elderlies often need more medical care.
  2. The retirement age should raise to 70.
  3. The number of older people are increasing.

12. Public transport and private cars

Question type: discuss both views and give your opinion

Practice question

Some people believe that governments should invest more money in public transport, while others think that improving roads should be the priority.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Ideas to consider

  • Efficient public transport may reduce traffic congestion.
  • Public transport can improve accessibility for people who cannot drive.
  • Roads remain essential for businesses and rural communities.
  • Integrated transport planning could support both needs.

Useful vocabulary

  • traffic congestion: too many vehicles on the roads. Traffic congestion costs businesses time and money.
  • transport infrastructure: roads, railways and stations. Good transport infrastructure attracts investment.
  • accessibility: how easy it is for everyone to use something. Low-floor buses improve accessibility for wheelchair users.
  • public investment: money the government spends on public projects. Rail projects need long-term public investment.
  • rural communities: people living in the countryside. Rural communities depend on good roads.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: vary your key words

Repeating the same phrase many times can limit your vocabulary score. Find two other ways to refer to each:

  1. public transport
  2. improving roads
  3. the government

13. Cultural traditions and globalisation

Question type: positive or negative development?

Practice question

As people around the world adopt increasingly similar lifestyles, local traditions and customs are becoming less important.

Is this a positive or negative development?

Ideas to consider

  • Shared lifestyles can encourage communication and cultural exchange.
  • Globalisation can create educational and employment opportunities.
  • The loss of traditions may weaken cultural identity.
  • Traditional knowledge may be difficult to recover once it is lost.

Useful vocabulary

  • cultural identity: a sense of belonging to a culture. Language is central to cultural identity.
  • globalisation: the world becoming more connected. Globalisation has made foreign food widely available.
  • cultural exchange: sharing ideas and customs between cultures. Student exchanges encourage cultural exchange.
  • traditional customs: long-established ways of doing things. Some traditional customs survive mainly in villages.
  • cultural diversity: many different cultures existing together. Cultural diversity makes cities more interesting.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: answer the actual question

This question asks whether the development is positive or negative, not whether you agree. Complete this position sentence:

"Although ______, I believe this is largely a ______ development because ______."

14. Food waste

Question type: causes and solutions (two questions)

Practice question

Large quantities of food are wasted by households, businesses and supermarkets.

Why does this happen, and what can be done to reduce food waste?

Ideas to consider

  • Consumers sometimes purchase more food than they need.
  • People may misunderstand food expiry labels.
  • Supermarkets may reject products because of their appearance.
  • Better meal planning and food redistribution schemes could reduce waste.

Useful vocabulary

  • food waste: food that is thrown away. Households produce a large share of food waste.
  • surplus food: extra food that hasn't been sold or eaten. Supermarkets can donate surplus food to charities.
  • the supply chain: all the steps from producer to consumer. Food is lost at every stage of the supply chain.
  • food redistribution: giving unused food to people who need it. Food redistribution apps connect cafés with local residents.
  • consumer awareness: how much shoppers know about an issue. Campaigns can raise consumer awareness of food waste.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: cause and effect language

Complete the sentences with: due to, as a result, which leads to

  1. Many people buy in bulk to save money, ______ food going off before it is eaten.
  2. Some fruit is rejected ______ its unusual shape.
  3. Customers misunderstand "best before" dates. ______, they throw away food that is still safe to eat.

15. Success and the purpose of education

Question type: discuss both views and give your opinion

Practice question

Some people believe that the main purpose of education is to prepare individuals for employment. Others believe that education should focus on personal development.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Ideas to consider

  • Employment-related skills can improve graduates' career prospects.
  • Education can develop critical thinking and independence.
  • Personal development benefits individuals beyond the workplace.
  • Academic and vocational learning can support both objectives.

Useful vocabulary

  • employability: how attractive you are to employers. Internships improve graduates' employability.
  • personal development: growing as a person. University is a time of personal development as well as study.
  • critical thinking: judging ideas carefully and logically. Critical thinking is useful in every career.
  • vocational training: training for a specific job or trade. Vocational training can lead directly to skilled work.
  • intellectual curiosity: a desire to learn and understand. Good teachers encourage intellectual curiosity.

Practise this vocabulary: interactive matching activity

Quick practice: which conclusion is better?

A: "In conclusion, education should prepare people for work and help them grow as individuals. Also, governments should make university free for everyone."

B: "In conclusion, although preparing students for employment is important, I believe education should also develop critical thinking and independence, as these benefit people throughout their lives."

How to develop your ideas for a Band 7 essay

Having ideas is important, but knowing how to develop them matters even more.

Here's an example from the topic of remote working.

Underdeveloped idea:

Working from home is good because people save time. They can spend more time with their families.

More developed idea:

One advantage of remote working is that employees no longer need to spend several hours each week commuting. This gives them more time to manage their personal responsibilities, such as preparing meals or looking after their children. Consequently, working from home may help employees maintain a healthier work-life balance.

The second example is stronger because the writer explains the link between the main idea and its consequences.

You don't need an exceptionally original argument. You need a relevant argument that is clearly explained and supported.

What should you practise before your IELTS Writing exam?

Instead of writing 15 complete essays straight away, try this approach for each topic:

  1. Identify the essay question type.
  2. Underline the key words in the question.
  3. Decide on your position.
  4. Write your introduction with an outline that names your two main ideas.
  5. Plan the main idea for each body paragraph, and explain and support it.
  6. Do the quick practice activity for that topic.
  7. Once a week, write a full essay under timed conditions.

In the IELTS Writing test, you should spend about 40 minutes on Task 2 and write at least 250 words.

When you check your essay, think about all four IELTS criteria: Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

Should you memorise IELTS essay topics?

It's helpful to become familiar with common themes and topic vocabulary. However, memorising complete essays is not an effective preparation strategy.

Even a small change in the wording of a question can change what you need to discuss.

Practise responding to different questions on the same topic. For example, an essay about the problems social media causes for young people needs a different approach from one about whether governments should regulate social media companies.

The more flexible your writing skills become, the better prepared you'll be for an unfamiliar question.
