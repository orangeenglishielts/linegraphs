Describing pie charts: proportions, the passive and comparisons

Build accurate sentences one step at a time, from a simple statement to a full comparison with data.

How this lesson works

You will build sentences about pie charts one step at a time: first a simple, accurate sentence, then the passive, then the figures, then comparisons, and finally all of these together.

  1. A simple sentenceHousehold waste was recycled.
  2. Add how much34% of household waste was recycled.
  3. Say it another wayJust over a third of household waste was recycled.
  4. CompareMore household waste was recycled than sent to landfill.
  5. Put it all togetherJust over a third of household waste was recycled, compared with around a quarter that was sent to landfill.

            Step 3: Use the passive

            Many pie charts show what happened to something: how waste was dealt with, how money was spent, how energy was produced. We usually don’t know or care who did it, so the passive is the natural choice.

            Active: The city recycled 34% of its household waste.
            Passive: 34% of household waste was recycled.

            Form: was / were + past participle. Use was with uncountable nouns like waste, energy and money, and with “34% of the waste”. Use the past if the chart gives a past year.

                  Step 5: Add the data

                  A strong comparison gives the reader the figures too. Add them in brackets, after a colon, after compared with, or in a second sentence starting with Specifically,. You can also contrast two figures with whereas or while.

                      Your turn

                      Look at the international students chart again. Write an overview and one body paragraph (about 100 words). Use at least three different comparison structures, add the figures, and say what each percentage is a percentage of.

                      Overall, China and India were by far the largest sources of international students, together accounting for half of the total, while each of the other countries made up a much smaller share.

                      China accounted for the largest proportion, at 28%, followed by India with just over a fifth (22%). Russia was a distant third: at 12%, its share was less than half that of China. As many students came from Brazil as from Germany, with each sending 10% of the total. Nigeria provided the smallest proportion, just 6%, which was half the figure for Russia. The remaining 12% came from other countries.

                      Before you finish, ask

                      1. Percentage of what? Is the whole the same as the chart title?
                      2. Are my fractions precise: just over, just under, almost?
                      3. Did I use the passive where nobody did the action?
                      4. Did I compare the slices with a range of structures?
                      5. Did I add the figures to support each comparison?
