Overall, the younger group spent most of their free time on social media, while the older group watched far more television than they spent on anything else. The over-60s spent more time than the under-30s on three of the five activities.

      Step 2: Paraphrase the question

      Your first sentence rewrites the task in your own words. Change the words and the word order, but keep every fact: what is measured, who, where and when. Copied words don’t count towards your 150, and a copied introduction tells the examiner nothing about your English.

      Useful changes: shows → compares, illustrates, gives a breakdown of · the number of → how many · the percentage of → the proportion of, the share of · from 2010 to 2025 → over a fifteen-year period · in three countries → name them. Never write “below”: your reader can’t see the task.

        Step 3: Write the overview

        The overview is the most important paragraph for Task Achievement. It comes straight after the introduction, starts with Overall, and gives the two or three biggest features in one or two sentences, with no figures.

        Static chart: the largest and smallest categories, and the biggest difference.
        Dynamic chart: the overall trend (up, down, stable), the biggest change, and whether the ranking changed or stayed the same.
        No clear trend? That is the main feature, so say it: the figures fluctuated, with no clear upward or downward trend. Then add what is clear: which was always highest, which was most stable, or whether the figures ended where they started. Never leave the overview out.

        Step 4: Organise the body paragraphs

        Don’t describe the chart from left to right. Choose a plan that groups the information logically, so each body paragraph has one clear job. These are the five most useful plans.

        By time (dynamic charts)Body 1: the first year and the ranking. Body 2: the changes and the final year.
        By size (static charts)Body 1: the largest categories. Body 2: the smaller ones.
        By trendBody 1: the categories that rose. Body 2: those that fell or stayed the same.
        By groupBody 1: where one group was higher. Body 2: where the other was.
        By chart (two different charts)Body 1: the first chart. Body 2: the second, linked back to the first.

            Step 5: Signpost your plan

            Once you have a plan, show it. Start each body paragraph with a signpost that tells the reader what the paragraph is about. This is what examiners mean by clear progression, and it is part of Coherence and Cohesion.

                Step 7: Your turn

                Task · 20 minutes · at least 150 words

                The table below gives information about the percentage of households with internet access in four regions in 2010 and 2025.

                Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

                The table compares the proportion of households in four regions that had internet access in 2010 and 2025.

                Overall, internet access rose considerably in every region, and the differences between the regions became much smaller over the period.

                In 2010, the West had the highest proportion of connected households, at 70%, followed by the South (62%). The figures for the North and East were much lower, at 45% and 38% respectively, so the gap between the highest and lowest regions was 32 percentage points.

                By 2025, at least 85% of households in every region had internet access. The East saw the largest increase, more than doubling to 85%, while the North almost doubled to 88%. The figures for the South and West rose more slowly, to 91% and 90% respectively, and the gap between the regions had narrowed to just 6 percentage points.

                Before you finish, ask

                1. Select: did I choose the main features, not every figure?
                2. Describe: is the tense right for the dates? Does every figure have its unit?
                3. Compare: does every body paragraph compare?
                4. Structure: introduction, overview, two body paragraphs?
