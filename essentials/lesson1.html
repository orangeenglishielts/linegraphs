    How Task 1 is marked

    Your answer gets a band for each of four criteria, with equal weight: Task Achievement (did you select, describe and compare accurately, with an overview?), Coherence and Cohesion (organisation, paragraphs, linking), Lexical Resource (vocabulary and spelling) and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1, so keep Task 1 to about 20 minutes.

    Step 2: Static or dynamic?

    Before you write, ask: does this chart show one moment or change over time? It isn’t about the chart type. A pie chart, a bar chart or a table can show either. Look for dates in the title, on the axis or in the column headings.

    Static (one point in time): compare the categories. the largest share, higher than, twice as many as.
    Dynamic (two or more points in time): describe the changes, then compare them. rose, fell, doubled, overtook, remained stable.

    Pie charts

    Static

    Household spending, 2025

    One year: compare the slices. Housing accounted for the largest share.

    Dynamic

    Household spending, 2005 and 2025

    Two years: describe the change. Housing rose from 20% to 35%.

    Bar charts

    Static

    Coffee consumption by country, 2024

    Countries on the axis: compare. Finland drank the most coffee.

    Dynamic

    Coffee consumption in the UK, 2000–2024

    Years on the axis: change over time. Consumption rose, then levelled off.

        Step 4: Describe

        Describe the data, not the picture: people, money, hours or tonnes, not bars, lines or colours. Then check two things that cost easy marks: the tense and the units.

        The chart or the data?

        0 / 6

        Does each sentence describe the picture or the data?

        Which tense?

        Past years: past simple (rose, accounted for).
        Future years or a projection: a future form (is expected to, is predicted to, will).
        No date, such as a process: present simple (is poured).
        From the past until now: present perfect (has been built).
        Look at the dates in the title and on the axis before you write.

        Units and measures

        Check the axis or column heading for the unit and give it every time: %, hours, litres, tonnes, US$. If the axis says (millions), write 10 million, not 10 or 10 millions. Use the number of for things you can count and the amount of for things you can’t.

        Subject–verb agreement

        Find the head noun of the subject and make the verb agree with it, not with the nearest noun.
        The number of visitors was… (head noun: number) · The percentage of households has risen
        With a percentage or fraction, agree with the noun after of: 28% of students were but 34% of the waste was.
        Each, every, one of → singular. Both, a number of → plural. The chart shows, the charts show.

          Uncountable nouns

          Many Task 1 topics are uncountable: water, electricity, energy, money, spending, waste, information, traffic, equipment, unemployment, rainfall.
          They have no plural -s (informations ✗), take a singular verb (waste was), and use the amount of, less and much instead of the number of, fewer and many.
          To count them, add a unit: litres of water, tonnes of waste, kilowatt-hours of electricity.
