Workbook: Task 1 Adjectives

Learn the precise vocabulary examiners want to see when comparing data

Why This Matters
In Task 1, you don't describe "hot" or "beautiful" things. You describe how data changes. The adjectives below are what examiners expect to see. They show you understand the data and can use sophisticated vocabulary precisely.

This workbook has four sections. Each one teaches you a category of adjectives, then tests your memory of what each word means. At the end, you'll have a reference card to print and keep.

Tip: Look for time clues! "In just two years" = rapid/sharp/dramatic. "Throughout the decade" = gradual/steady.
Exercise: Complete these sentences
Sales barely moved. There was only a increase in the period.
Visitor numbers exploded. A rise occurred from 1 million to 8 million in just two years.
Over the entire decade, tourism climbed reliably. It showed growth year after year.

Key rule: Superlatives always need THE. "The highest point," "The most significant change," "The largest increase."
Exercise: Complete with superlatives
The point on the graph was 8.5 million visitors.
The change in sales happened between 2015 and 2016.
The increase was in urban areas.

Pattern & Stability
How consistent or unpredictable the trend is
volatile
stable
consistent
steady
erratic
fluctuating
Tip: Volatile and erratic = unpredictable ups and downs. Stable and consistent = predictable and reliable.
Exercise: Complete these sentences
The data showed a pattern, with unpredictable jumps up and down.
Coffee consumption remained at around 3kg per capita throughout the decade.
Sales showed growth every single year with no dips.
Test Your Memory: Match words to meanings
volatile
unstable, unpredictable ups and downs
erratic
irregular and unpredictable
stable
not changing, steady and safe
