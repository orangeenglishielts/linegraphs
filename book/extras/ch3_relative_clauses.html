Relative clauses for describing data

Join figures, times and comparisons into one accurate sentence, the way Band 7+ answers do.

Why relative clauses matter at Band 7

At Band 7, the examiner looks for a variety of complex structures, with most sentences free of errors. Relative clauses are one of the most useful complex structures in Task 1: they let you attach a figure, a time or a comparison to a noun without starting a new sentence. That turns a list of short facts into connected description.

Band 6: Cycling rose to 34% in 2020. This was its highest figure. Car use fell to 37%. The gap became three points.
Band 7+: Cycling rose to 34% in 2020, which was its highest figure, while car use fell to 37%, narrowing the gap to just three points.

Six patterns for describing data

Defining · which / that · no commasThe town that recorded the highest water use was Northport.Tells the reader which one you mean.
Non-defining · , which … ,Gas, which overtook coal between 2008 and 2012, peaked at 42%.Adds extra information. Commas on both sides, and never “that”.
Whole-clause · , which …Car use fell to 37%, which narrowed the gap to three points.“Which” refers to the whole idea before it. Use it for what happened in the data, not for reasons the graph doesn’t show.
Time · when / during whichThe sharpest rise came between 2010 and 2015, when cycling grew by 7 points.For years and periods, use “when”, not “where”.
Possession · whoseBrazil, whose figure rose ninefold, still ended in last place.Works for countries, cities and companies, not just people.
Quantifier · of whichThe graph shows three sources, only one of which grew throughout.Also: both of which, all of which, the largest of which, neither of which.
Shortcut: a participle clause can replace “which” + verb. …, which narrowed the gap becomes …, narrowing the gap. The figure which was recorded in 2020 becomes the figure recorded in 2020.
Two or three per answer. Two or three relative clauses in an answer show range. Two “which” clauses in a row usually show confusion. Aim for two or three across the whole answer, not one in every sentence.

Your turn

Rewrite this body paragraph in three or four sentences. Use at least two different relative or participle clauses.

In 2009, 20% of South Koreans shopped online every week. This was the highest figure of the three countries. The UK figure was 15%. Both figures rose steeply until 2019. In 2019 South Korea reached 70% and the UK reached 55%. Growth then slowed. By 2024 the figures were 80% and 68%.
In 2009, South Korea, which had the highest figure of the three countries, had 20% of people shopping online every week, compared with 15% in the UK. Both figures rose steeply until 2019, when South Korea reached 70% and the UK 55%. Growth then slowed in both countries, with the figures reaching 80% and 68% respectively by 2024.

Check every relative clause

  1. Does it say which one (no commas) or add extra information (commas)?
  2. After a comma: which, who, whose, when. Never that.
  3. Is it right next to the noun it describes?
  4. Did I repeat the subject (“which it”)? Remove it.
  5. Is “where” really a place? For years, use “when”.
