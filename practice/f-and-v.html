F and V: Minimal Pairs Practice

Minimal pairs are two words that differ by one sound, like fan and van. Change the sound and you change the word. Press a word to hear me say it, repeat it, then press Check at the end of each exercise.

Sound isn't available in this browser. Ask your teacher to say the words, or open the page in Chrome or Safari.

How to make the sounds

/f/fan, leaf

Put your top teeth lightly on your bottom lip and blow air. No voice.

/v/van, leave

Same mouth position, but turn your voice on. Your lip should buzz.

Feel the difference: put your hand on your throat. Say ffff, then vvvv. You should feel a buzz only on /v/.

At the end of a word: the vowel before /v/ is a little longer. Compare safe (short) and save (longer).

Tips for Chinese speakers

  • /v/ is not /w/. For /w/ your lips are round and don't touch your teeth. For /v/ your top teeth touch your bottom lip. Compare vest / west, vet / wet, vine / wine.
  • /v/ is not /f/. If very sounds like ferry, add voice. Start with a long buzzing vvv, then say the rest: vvv-ery.
  • Don't drop the last sound. Finish leave, five and prove with a gentle buzz. Don't add a vowel after it, so say leave, not leave-uh.
