The Overview Sentence: The Single Line That Decides Your Task 1 Band

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 9 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

The chart shows household spending: housing accounts for 40%, groceries 30%, transport 20% and utilities 10%.

Warm-up: Which overview would you choose?

A. Overall, housing accounted for the largest share, while utilities represented the smallest.

B. Overall, housing was 40%, groceries were 30%, transport was 20% and utilities were 10%.

C. I think the chart shows that households spend too much on housing.

Best answer: A. It identifies the largest and smallest categories and makes a clear comparison. B reports figures but does not summarise the main pattern. C adds a personal opinion that the chart cannot support.

What the overview does, and why it matters

An overview is a brief summary of the chart’s most important features. It tells the reader what stands out before the detailed figures appear. In a line graph, that might be the overall direction of change and a key contrast. In a pie chart, it is often the largest and smallest shares.

The overview matters because Task Achievement assesses how well you address the task and highlight its key features. The official IELTS Writing band descriptors describe a clear overview and identification of main trends or differences at Band 7. Without a clear, relevant overview, reaching that level for Task Achievement becomes substantially harder.

A report can contain accurate figures and still leave the examiner searching for the main message. Put the overview in its own paragraph after the introduction, where it is easy to find. Keep exact figures for the body paragraphs: the overview summarises; the body supports it with data.

Spot the difference: weak summaries and strong overviews

Here are three common answers to the household pie chart.

A list of figures:

Housing was 40%, groceries were 30%, transport was 20% and utilities were 10%.

This sentence gives data, but it makes no selection. It reads like a shortened version of the chart’s labels. Move the figures into the body and use the overview to identify the main relationship.

A copy of the introduction:

The pie chart shows household expenditure in four categories.

This describes the visual but tells the reader nothing about its main features. It belongs, if suitably paraphrased, in the introduction, not the overview.

An unsupported opinion:

Overall, housing costs were far too high.

“Far too high” is a judgement. The chart provides no standard for deciding what counts as “too high”. Report what the data shows, not what you think it means.

Now compare two stronger versions:

Overall, housing accounted for the largest share of household spending, whereas utilities made up the smallest.

Why it works: it selects the two most noticeable ends of the comparison and links them clearly. It uses no exact figures.

Overall, housing and groceries together made up most household expenditure, while transport and utilities accounted for smaller proportions.

Why it works: it groups related categories to show the broad pattern. “Most” and “smaller proportions” summarise the data in words rather than listing percentages. Use this kind of grouping only when the chart supports it.

An effective overview does not need elaborate vocabulary or a complex sentence. It needs an accurate selection and a clear comparison.

Write one or two sentences in the right place

For most charts, one or two overview sentences are enough. Place them after the introduction and before the detailed body paragraphs.

Use one sentence when a single main pattern is clear:

Overall, the proportion travelling by bus declined, while cycling became more popular.

Use two when you need to cover both the overall pattern and a major contrast:

Overall, spending on education rose across the period. The increase was strongest in the final years, while health expenditure remained relatively stable.

Do not turn the overview into a second body paragraph. If it includes a sequence of exact dates, percentages or values, it is carrying too much detail. Keep those figures for the body.

A useful formula for each chart type

  • Line graph: overall trend + important contrast or high/low point.

Overall, both figures increased, although the rise in online sales was considerably steeper.

  • Pie chart: largest and smallest shares, or a clear grouping.

Overall, food made up the largest share, while transport represented the smallest.

  • Bar chart: highest and lowest categories, or the main pattern across groups.

Overall, younger adults recorded the highest participation, while the oldest group had the lowest.

  • Table: the biggest comparison or standout pattern.

Overall, the northern region had the highest totals in most categories, while the southern region recorded the lowest.

  • Process diagram: number of stages, where it starts and where it ends.

Overall, the process has five stages, beginning with the collection of raw materials and ending with the finished product being packaged.

  • Map: the most significant changes. What appeared, disappeared or changed use.

Overall, the area became more residential, with new housing replacing most of the open land.

Keep exact figures in the body

An overview normally describes patterns without exact numbers. Instead of writing:

Overall, the figure rose from 20 in 2010 to 60 in 2025.

write:

Overall, the figure rose considerably over the period.

Then use the body paragraph to report the relevant values. If a proportion is especially useful to summarise, wording such as approximately two fifths can work, but exact figures usually belong in the detailed report. For more practice with fractions and proportions, work through the companion lesson, Describing Percentages and Proportions in IELTS Writing Task 1.

The same principle applies to vocabulary. Choose words that match the data: a figure may rise gradually, remain stable or fluctuate. Avoid stronger verbs such as soar unless the change is genuinely dramatic. Accurate, familiar language is more effective than an impressive word used inaccurately. Review gradable adjectives and complex sentence structures as separate skills; neither can rescue an overview that misses the main feature.

Practise: repair, write and sort

Activity: fix four flawed overviews

For each example, identify the problem and rewrite the overview. The descriptions below provide the chart information.

  1. Line graph: both figures increased; one rose steadily and the other rose sharply near the end.

Overall, the first figure was 15 in 2010 and 45 in 2025, while the second went from 10 to 50.

  1. Pie chart: leisure was the largest category; savings was the smallest.

Overall, the chart shows how people used their monthly income.

  1. Bar chart: the oldest age group had the lowest participation; the two youngest groups were similar.

Overall, participation by the oldest group was disappointingly low.

  1. Process: six stages, from collecting coffee beans to packaging the finished product.

Overall, the beans are collected, cleaned, dried, roasted, ground and packaged.

Show answers

  1. Overall, both figures increased, although the second rose more sharply towards the end. The original lists data instead of summarising the trends.
  2. Overall, leisure accounted for the largest share, while savings represented the smallest. The original only introduces the visual.
  3. Overall, participation was lowest in the oldest group, while the two youngest groups recorded similar levels. “Disappointingly” adds an unsupported opinion.
  4. Overall, the process consists of six stages, beginning with the collection of coffee beans and ending with the finished product being packaged. The original recounts every step instead of summarising the process.

Activity: write an overview for a line graph

Imagine a graph of daily bicycle journeys in City A and City B between 2010 and 2025. Journeys in City A rise steadily throughout. City B starts higher, falls gradually and ends below City A.

Write one or two overview sentences. Include the overall trends and a meaningful comparison, but no exact figures.

Model answer

Overall, bicycle journeys increased steadily in City A, whereas they declined in City B. City A consequently overtook City B during the period.

The model captures the contrasting trends and the change in ranking. Only include “overtook” if the graph shows that the two lines cross.

Activity: overview or body detail?

Sort each sentence into overview or body detail.

  • A. Overall, online purchases increased, while in-store sales declined.
  • B. Online purchases rose from 15% in 2010 to 48% in 2020.
  • C. The process consists of four stages, beginning with sorting and ending with delivery.
  • D. In 2020, the northern region produced 12,000 tonnes.

Show answers: A and C are overview sentences. B and D give exact figures and belong in the body.

Make study-buddy practice count

Choose a chart and write an overview independently. Swap with a study buddy and assess each other’s work against this five-point checklist:

  1. Does it identify the most important feature or overall pattern?
  2. Does it include a useful comparison, where relevant?
  3. Is every claim supported by the chart?
  4. Does it avoid unnecessary exact figures and personal opinions?
  5. Is it placed after the introduction and easy to identify?

Give one specific improvement, then rewrite your overview. This quick exchange trains you to recognise the difference between reporting data and selecting the main features.

Build a clear overview

When you plan your next response, ask: What would I tell someone who could not see the chart? Your answer should capture the dominant trend, contrast or change, not reproduce the labels.

The overview is short, but it carries a high-value summary of your reading of the chart. Practise selecting the main features first; add precise figures in the body afterwards.
