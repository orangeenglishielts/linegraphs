Describing Percentages and Proportions in IELTS Writing Task 1

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 6 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

When describing charts in IELTS Academic Writing Task 1, you will frequently need to report percentages and proportions.

For example, you might need to describe:

  • The percentage of people who prefer different forms of transport.
  • The proportion of household income spent on housing.
  • The share of electricity generated from renewable sources.
  • The distribution of students across different academic subjects.

Although percentages are straightforward to understand, describing them accurately in English can be surprisingly challenging.

Should you write 25% or a quarter?

What’s the difference between the majority and the vast majority?

Can you use accounts for, constitutes and makes up interchangeably?

And what’s the difference between makes up and is made up of?

In this lesson, we’ll explore the language you need to describe percentages and proportions accurately and naturally. You’ll also have plenty of opportunities to practise.

1. Understanding percentages and proportions

Before learning different expressions, it’s important to understand what a percentage represents.

A percentage describes a quantity as a proportion of 100.

For example:

  • 25% = 25 out of every 100 = one quarter

Similarly:

  • 50% = half
  • 75% = three quarters
  • 20% = one fifth

In IELTS Writing Task 1, you can often express the same figure in different ways.

Consider these sentences:

Food accounts for 25% of household expenditure.
Food accounts for a quarter of household expenditure.

Both sentences communicate the same information. However, varying the way you express numerical information can help you avoid unnecessary repetition.

2. Using fractions instead of percentages

One of the simplest ways to vary your writing is to replace certain percentages with fractions.

Here are some useful equivalents.

  • 10%: One tenth
  • 20%: One fifth
  • 25%: One quarter
  • 30%: Three tenths
  • 33.3%: Approximately one third
  • 40%: Two fifths
  • 50%: Half
  • 60%: Three fifths
  • 66.7%: Approximately two thirds
  • 75%: Three quarters
  • 80%: Four fifths
  • 90%: Nine tenths

Some fractions are more commonly used than others. For example, a quarter, a third and half are extremely useful in IELTS Writing Task 1.

Other expressions, such as three tenths, are mathematically correct but less commonly used in everyday descriptions of data.

Examples

Percentage:

Housing accounts for 40% of total expenditure.

Fraction:

Housing accounts for two fifths of total expenditure.

Percentage:

Food represents 25% of the household budget.

Fraction:

Food represents a quarter of the household budget.

Percentage:

Transport accounts for 20% of expenditure.

Fraction:

Transport accounts for one fifth of expenditure.

3. Using approximations

Sometimes, a percentage is close to a familiar fraction but not exactly equal to it.

For example:

24% is close to 25%.

You could write:

Almost a quarter.

Or:

Just under a quarter.

Similarly, 26% could be described as:

Just over a quarter.

Useful approximation vocabulary

  • Approximately: Around a particular figure
  • About: Around a particular figure
  • Roughly: Around a particular figure
  • Nearly: Slightly less than a figure
  • Almost: Slightly less than a figure
  • Just under: Slightly less than a figure
  • Just over: Slightly more than a figure
  • Slightly more than: A little above a figure
  • Slightly less than: A little below a figure

Be careful with almost and just over

Compare:

Almost 50%

This means slightly less than half.

Just over 50%

This means slightly more than half.

The difference is important. If a chart shows 52%, you should not write:

Almost half of respondents…

Instead, you could write:

Just over half of respondents…

4. Describing large proportions

You don’t always need to use an exact percentage to describe a large proportion.

English has several expressions that communicate the relative size of a group. For example:

  • The majority
  • A clear majority
  • A substantial majority
  • The vast majority
  • An overwhelming majority
  • A significant proportion
  • A considerable proportion
  • A large share

However, these expressions are not interchangeable.

The majority

A majority means more than half.

The majority of respondents preferred online learning.

This tells us that more than 50% selected online learning. However, it does not tell us exactly how many.

A slight majority

A slight majority is only a little more than half.

Just 52% of respondents preferred online learning, representing a slight majority.

The vast majority

The vast majority refers to a very large proportion.

The vast majority of respondents (92%) preferred online learning.

This is an appropriate use because the figure is very high. However, describing 55% as the vast majority would be misleading.

An overwhelming majority

An overwhelming majority suggests that one group greatly outnumbers the alternative.

An overwhelming majority of respondents (96%) supported the proposal.

A substantial proportion

A substantial proportion is a relatively large share.

Housing accounts for a substantial proportion of household expenditure, at 40%.

Notice that a substantial proportion does not necessarily mean a majority.

Important: There are no fixed numerical thresholds for expressions such as substantial, significant or vast majority. Their appropriateness depends on the context.

5. Describing small proportions

Just as we can describe large proportions, we can also describe relatively small shares.

Useful expressions include:

  • A minority
  • A small minority
  • A relatively small proportion
  • A small fraction
  • A tiny fraction
  • A negligible proportion
  • A marginal share

A minority

A minority is a group representing less than half of a population.

A minority of respondents preferred travelling by train.

However, minority does not necessarily mean a very small proportion. A group representing 45% is still technically a minority if the remaining 55% belong to another group.

A small minority

This expression indicates that relatively few people belong to a particular group.

Only 8% of respondents preferred travelling by bicycle, representing a small minority.

A tiny fraction

This expression emphasises that a proportion is extremely small.

Only 2% of respondents selected the final option, representing a tiny fraction of the total.

A negligible proportion

Negligible suggests that a proportion is so small that it has little practical significance in the context.

The final category accounted for a negligible proportion of total expenditure, at just 0.5%.

Be careful with this expression. A figure of 5% might be relatively small, but it is not necessarily negligible.

6. Useful verbs for describing proportions

One of the most useful ways to vary your IELTS Writing Task 1 sentences is to use different verbs.

Consider this sentence:

Housing is 40% of total expenditure.

Although the meaning is understandable, we can express it more naturally.

Housing accounts for 40% of total expenditure.

There are several alternatives.

Account for

Structure:

Category + accounts for + percentage + of + total

Example:

Housing accounts for 40% of household expenditure.

This is one of the most useful expressions for describing proportions.

Constitute

Structure:

Category + constitutes + percentage + of + total

Example:

Housing constitutes 40% of total expenditure.

Constitute means to form or make up part of something. It is relatively formal and appropriate for academic writing.

Represent

Structure:

Category + represents + percentage + of + total

Example:

Food represents 25% of the household budget.

This is another natural way to express proportions.

Make up

Structure:

Category + makes up + percentage + of + total

Example:

Transport makes up 20% of household expenditure.

Make up is a phrasal verb meaning to form or constitute part of something. It is common in descriptions of statistical information.

Comprise

Structure:

Category + comprises + percentage + of + total

Example:

Housing comprises 40% of the total budget.

This usage is possible, although accounts for and constitutes are often more natural when reporting a percentage.

Comprise is especially useful when describing the components of a whole.

The household budget comprises five categories.

7. Make up vs be made up of

This is an important grammatical distinction. Although these expressions look similar, they describe different relationships.

Make up

Use make up when describing how much a category contributes to a whole.

Housing makes up 40% of household expenditure.

Here, housing is one component of the total.

Be made up of

Use be made up of when describing the components that form a whole.

Household expenditure is made up of five categories.

Here, household expenditure is the whole. The five categories are its components.

Compare

PART → WHOLE

Housing makes up 40% of total expenditure.

WHOLE → PARTS

Total expenditure is made up of housing, food, transport, entertainment and other expenses.

The difference is grammatical as well as conceptual.

Consist of

Consist of is another useful expression for describing composition.

The household budget consists of five categories.

Comprise

Comprise can also describe the composition of a whole.

The household budget comprises five categories.

In standard formal usage, comprise is generally used without of.

Correct:

The budget comprises five categories.

Incorrect in standard formal usage:

The budget comprises of five categories.

8. Describing comparisons between percentages

In addition to describing individual proportions, you may need to compare them.

Consider the household expenditure chart:

  • Housing: 40%
  • Transport: 20%

Housing accounts for twice the proportion represented by transport.

Alternatively:

The proportion spent on housing is twice as large as that spent on transport.

Twice as large as

Use this expression when one figure is double another.

The proportion spent on housing (40%) is twice as large as that spent on transport (20%).

Three times as large as

Use this expression when one figure is three times another. For example, if one category accounts for 60% and another for 20%:

The larger category accounts for three times the proportion of the smaller category.

Slightly higher than

Use this when the difference is relatively small.

Food accounts for a slightly higher proportion than transport, at 25% and 20%, respectively.

Considerably higher than

Use this when the difference is substantial.

Housing accounts for a considerably higher proportion than entertainment, at 40% and 10%, respectively.

Four times as large as

Compare:

  • Housing: 40%
  • Entertainment: 10%
The proportion spent on housing is four times as large as that spent on entertainment.

A note about percentage points

Be careful when describing differences between percentages.

If one category accounts for 40% and another accounts for 20%, the difference is:

20 percentage points

The larger proportion is also twice the smaller one. These are two different ways of describing the relationship.

Activity: percentage points or per cent?

The share of household spending on transport rose from 20% to 25%. Is each description accurate?

The share rose by 5 percentage points.

Accurate. 25 minus 20 is 5, and this is the clearest way to describe the change.

The share rose by 5%.

Not accurate. A 5% rise would take 20 only to 21. The change here is 5 percentage points.

The share rose by a quarter.

Accurate. 5 is a quarter of 20, so the share became a quarter larger.

The share rose by 25%.

Accurate, but take care. 5 ÷ 20 = 0.25, so this is correct as a relative increase. Many readers will find it confusing, so percentage points are usually safer.

9. Combining vocabulary and comparisons

Now let’s use several of these expressions together.

Consider this basic sentence:

Housing is 40% and food is 25%.

We could write:

Housing accounts for two fifths of total expenditure, compared with a quarter allocated to food.

Alternatively:

Housing represents the largest share of expenditure, at 40%, while food constitutes a further 25%.

Or:

Together, housing and food make up almost two thirds of total expenditure, accounting for 65% of the budget.

These sentences communicate the information more effectively because they show relationships between categories.

However, remember that the purpose is not simply to include as many expressions as possible. Choose the structures that help you describe the data clearly.

10. Common mistakes to avoid

Mistake 1: Using majority incorrectly

Incorrect:

The majority of expenditure was allocated to housing (40%).

Forty per cent is not a majority.

Better:

Housing accounted for the largest proportion of expenditure, at 40%.

Mistake 2: Using vast majority for a relatively small majority

Incorrect:

The vast majority of respondents (55%) preferred online learning.

Better:

A slight majority of respondents (55%) preferred online learning.

Mistake 3: Confusing make up and be made up of

Incorrect:

Housing is made up of 40% of the budget.

Correct:

Housing makes up 40% of the budget.

Mistake 4: Incorrect verb agreement

Incorrect:

Housing account for 40% of expenditure.

Correct:

Housing accounts for 40% of expenditure.

Remember that a singular subject requires a singular verb.

Mistake 5: Using comprise of

Incorrect in standard formal usage:

The budget comprises of five categories.

Correct:

The budget comprises five categories.

Or:

The budget consists of five categories.

Mistake 6: Confusing percentages and percentage points

Incorrect:

Housing is 20% higher than transport.

Housing represents 40%, while transport represents 20%. Housing’s share is 20 percentage points higher, or twice as large.

Better:

Housing accounts for 20 percentage points more than transport.

Or:

The proportion spent on housing is twice as large as that spent on transport.

11. Final IELTS Writing Task 1 practice

Now it’s time to apply what you’ve learned.

Look at the household expenditure chart again. The pie chart shows how a household’s total expenditure is distributed across five categories.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, making comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.

Before writing, try to include:

  • At least two different verbs for describing proportions.
  • One fraction.
  • One comparison.
  • One sentence combining two categories.
  • A clear overview.

Model answer

The pie chart illustrates how a household’s total expenditure is divided among five categories: housing, food, transport, entertainment and other expenses.

Overall, housing accounts for the largest proportion of spending, while other expenses represent the smallest share. Together, housing and food constitute almost two thirds of the total budget.

Housing makes up two fifths of total expenditure, at 40%, making it the largest category. Food accounts for a further 25%, or one quarter of the budget. Combined, these two categories represent 65% of total household spending.

Transport constitutes one fifth of expenditure, at 20%. This is twice the proportion allocated to entertainment, which accounts for 10% of the budget. The remaining 5% is spent on other expenses, making this the smallest category.

In short, the chart shows that essential household costs, particularly housing, food and transport, account for the great majority of total expenditure.

Language analysis

Notice the expressions used in the model:

Accounts for Describes the proportion represented by housing.

Makes up Describes housing’s contribution to the total.

Constitute Describes the combined proportion represented by housing and food.

One fifth Provides an alternative to 20%.

Twice the proportion Compares transport and entertainment.

The smallest category Identifies a key feature.

The model also includes an overview and relevant comparisons.

Remember that using these expressions does not automatically guarantee a particular IELTS band score. The accuracy, relevance and organisation of your response are equally important.

Final thoughts

Describing percentages effectively in IELTS Writing Task 1 requires more than memorising alternative expressions. You need to understand the precise meaning of the vocabulary you use.

  • Is made up of describes the components of a whole.

Learning these distinctions will help you write more accurate and natural descriptions of pie charts, bar charts, tables and other visual information.

Most importantly, remember that good academic writing is not about using the most complicated vocabulary. It’s about choosing the right language to communicate the information clearly.
