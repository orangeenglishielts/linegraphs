Static Bar Charts in IELTS Writing Task 1: No Time Period

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 25 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

Some bar charts in IELTS Writing Task 1 show change over several years. Others show just one moment: one year, or no date at all. These are static charts, and they need a different approach.

As a former IELTS examiner, I'd point you to one question before you write anything: is there a time period on this chart? If there isn't, words like "increased" and "fell" don't belong in your answer, because nothing has changed. Your job is to compare.

How to spot a static bar chart

Look at the x-axis and the legend.

  • Dynamic chart: years or months along the axis, or a legend with different years (2010, 2015, 2020). You describe change.
  • Static chart: categories along the axis (countries, age groups, types of transport) and one year in the title, or no year. You describe differences.

Example chart: how men and women commuted to work in one city in 2022 (% of each group).

Step 1: Group before you write

With a static chart, the hardest part is deciding what to put together. Don't go bar by bar. Look for:

  • The biggest and smallest figures
  • Big differences between groups (car: 44% vs 31%)
  • Similar figures (train: 18% vs 17%)
  • Opposite patterns (men drove more; women walked and took the bus more)

For this chart, a clear plan is:

  • Body paragraph 1: car and bicycle, where men's figures were higher
  • Body paragraph 2: bus and walking, where women's figures were higher, and train, where they were almost the same

Step 2: Write an overview with no numbers

The overview tells the reader the main features. For a static chart, that usually means the largest category and the biggest contrast.

Overall, the car was the most common way to commute for both men and women, although men were more likely to drive, while women were more likely to take the bus or walk.

Step 3: Choose the tense

This is where static charts catch people out.

  • A past year in the title (2022): past simple. "44% of men drove to work."
  • No date: present simple. "44% of men drive to work."
  • A future year (2030): future forms. "44% of men are expected to drive."

Pick one and keep it for the whole answer.

Step 4: Use comparison language, not trend language

  • ✗ Car use increased to 44% for men. ✓ 44% of men travelled by car, compared with 31% of women.
  • ✗ Walking rose for women. ✓ Women were twice as likely as men to walk to work.
  • ✗ Train use stayed stable. ✓ The figures for train travel were almost identical.

For more phrases, see my post on IELTS bar chart vocabulary.

Model answer

The bar chart compares the ways in which men and women in one city travelled to work in 2022.

Overall, the car was the most common way to commute for both men and women, although men were more likely to drive, while women were more likely to take the bus or walk.

Men relied on the car considerably more than women did, at 44% compared with 31%, which was the largest gap between the two groups. Cycling showed a similar pattern on a smaller scale, with 16% of men cycling to work and 12% of women.

By contrast, women were more likely to use the bus, at 23% compared with 14% of men, and they were more than twice as likely to walk (17% compared with 8%). The train was the only option used by almost the same proportion of both groups, at 18% of men and 17% of women.

(151 words)

Notice what the answer doesn't do. It doesn't mention every number in order, and it doesn't use a single trend verb.

Practice activities

Activity 1: Static or dynamic?

Read each chart title. Is the chart static (compare) or dynamic (describe change)?

  1. Average household spending on five categories in Canada, 2019
  2. Car ownership in three countries, 1990 to 2020
  3. The favourite sports of boys and girls at one school
  4. The number of international students in four countries in 2005, 2010 and 2015
  5. The expected share of electricity from different sources in 2040

Answers (try first!): 1 static (past tense) · 2 dynamic · 3 static (present tense, no date) · 4 dynamic · 5 static (future forms: "is expected to")

Activity 2: From trend to comparison

Each sentence uses trend language for the commuting chart, which is static. Rewrite it as a comparison.

  1. Car use increased to 44% for men.
  2. Bus use went up for women.
  3. Cycling dropped for women.

Answers (try first!), example answers:

  1. 44% of men travelled to work by car, compared with 31% of women.
  2. Women were more likely than men to take the bus, at 23% and 14% respectively.
  3. Fewer women than men cycled to work, at 12% compared with 16%.

Quick checklist for static charts

  • I checked: is there a time period?
  • I grouped the data before writing
  • My overview has no numbers
  • My tense matches the date in the title
  • I used comparison language, not trend verbs
