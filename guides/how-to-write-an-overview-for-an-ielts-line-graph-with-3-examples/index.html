How to Write an Overview for an IELTS Line Graph (With 3 Examples)

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 9 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

The short answer: an overview is one or two sentences that sum up the main trends and the biggest differences in the graph, without specific numbers. It's what an examiner looks for first in Task 1, because without a clear overview your answer can't reach the higher bands for Task Achievement.

The graph

We'll use the practice graph at the top of this article (invented data). It shows the average number of bicycle trips made per day in three cities between 2000 and 2020. Northport rises from 20,000 to 65,000; Eastvale fluctuates between 38,000 and 46,000, ending at 42,000; Riverton falls from 55,000 to 25,000.

What an overview needs to do

Ask yourself: if someone could only read two sentences about this graph, what should they know? Usually that's:

  • the overall direction of each line (rising, falling, fluctuating, stable)
  • the biggest contrast or change in ranking
  • where the lines start and finish relative to each other

Keep the numbers for your body paragraphs.

Exercise: which overview is best?

Read all three, choose the best one, then check the reasoning.

A. Overall, Northport had 20,000 trips in 2000 and 65,000 in 2020, while Riverton went from 55,000 to 25,000.

B. Overall, the number of bicycle trips changed a lot in all three cities over the period.

C. Overall, cycling rose sharply in Northport and fell steadily in Riverton, so the two cities swapped places, while Eastvale's figures fluctuated but finished close to where they started. By 2020, Northport had the most bicycle trips and Riverton the fewest.

Best answer: C. It gives the direction of all three lines, picks out the most important feature (Northport and Riverton reversing their positions) and contains no specific numbers. A lists figures for two cities and ignores Eastvale. B tries to sum up the whole graph but says almost nothing: "changed a lot" would fit almost any line graph, and that's a warning sign.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the overview at the very end, after the reader has already worked through the details. It can go there, but I recommend putting it straight after your introduction, where the examiner is looking for it.
  • Including specific figures.
  • Describing only one or two of the lines.
  • Starting with "In conclusion". Task 1 is a report, not an essay, so "Overall" is the natural way to begin.

A quick checklist

  • Have I described the direction of every line?
  • Have I named the biggest change or contrast?
  • Have I kept specific numbers out?
  • Could my overview only describe this graph, or would it fit any graph?

Exercise: write an overview for a different graph

Imagine a line graph showing how people commuted in one city between 2005 and 2025 (invented data). Car travel falls from 60% to 35%. Train travel rises from 25% to 40%, overtaking the car near the end of the period. Cycling rises from 15% to 25%.

Write one or two overview sentences, with no specific numbers. Then check your answer.

Model answer

Overall, car travel became much less common, while train and bicycle commuting both grew. By the end of the period, the train had replaced the car as the most popular way to commute.
