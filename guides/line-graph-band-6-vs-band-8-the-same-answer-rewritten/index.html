Line Graph Band 6 vs Band 8: The Same Answer, Rewritten

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 3 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

A report can sound fluent and still miss the features that distinguish a Band 6 response from a stronger one. Try this warm-up: read the excerpt below and think about what’s holding it back.

“Trips increased a lot in the city centre. The figure went up every year, and it was very popular in the last year.”

Answer: this reads like Band 6-style writing, although a short excerpt can’t be given a real band. The meaning is clear, but the description is vague and does not compare the series or select the most important features. Higher-band writing is not about making every sentence sound complicated. It is about reporting the data accurately and organising it so the main story is easy to see.

The practice graph: one task, two reports

The fictional graph below was created for practice. It shows annual bike-share trips, in thousands, in three areas from 2019 to 2023.

  • 2019: City centre: 20; University area: 30; Riverside: 10
  • 2020: City centre: 25; University area: 32; Riverside: 15
  • 2021: City centre: 40; University area: 33; Riverside: 25
  • 2022: City centre: 45; University area: 32; Riverside: 30
  • 2023: City centre: 45; University area: 33; Riverside: 35

Task: The line graph shows the number of bike-share trips in three areas between 2019 and 2023. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

The examples are illustrative, not official IELTS scores. An examiner assesses a response across all four criteria, so no single feature guarantees a particular band. The official IELTS Writing band descriptors explain what the criteria require.

Band 6 version: understandable, but mainly a list

The line graph shows the number of bike-share trips in thousands in the city centre, university area and riverside from 2019 to 2023. Overall, the figures changed during the period and there were more trips at the end. The number of trips in the city centre was 20,000 in 2019. The figure went up to 25,000 in 2020 and then it rise to 40,000 in 2021. It went up to 45,000 in 2022 and stayed the same in 2023. The number of trips in the university area were 30,000 in 2019 and went up a little to 32,000 in 2020. It was 33,000 in 2021, went down to 32,000 in 2022 and increase again to 33,000 in 2023. For riverside, the number was 10,000 at the beginning. It went up to 15,000 and then 25,000 in the next years. In 2022 it was 30,000, and it has 35,000 trips in 2023. This is a lot more than before. The city centre was so much higher than riverside at the start, but the gap got smaller. All areas had more trips than in 2019.

Margin notes

  • No meaningful overview: “The figures changed” does not identify the main trends.
  • Number-by-number reporting: the details follow the graph’s timeline without grouping related trends.
  • Repetitive stems and flat verbs: “The number of” and “went up” recur instead of conveying the size or shape of a change.
  • Tense slips: “rise”, “increase” and “has” do not consistently match a finished period from 2019 to 2023.
  • Imprecise language: “a lot” and “so much higher” do not describe the data precisely.
  • Limited comparison: one gap is mentioned, but the areas are not clearly compared as a group.

The response meets the basic task and is understandable. The upgrade is not simply to add advanced vocabulary; it is to choose, group and compare the information more deliberately.

Band 8 version: the same data, organised around its main story

The line graph compares annual bike-share trips, measured in thousands, in the city centre, university area and riverside between 2019 and 2023. Overall, trips rose substantially in the city centre and riverside, while the university figure remained broadly stable. The city centre recorded the highest total by the end of the period, despite starting below the university area. City-centre trips climbed from 20,000 in 2019 to 25,000 the following year, before rising sharply to 40,000 in 2021. They then reached 45,000 in 2022 and levelled off. By comparison, the university figure changed very little: it edged up from 30,000 to 33,000 between 2019 and 2021, dipped slightly to 32,000 a year later and finished at 33,000. Riverside use increased steadily throughout the period, rising from 10,000 trips in 2019 to 35,000 in 2023. It more than tripled and, by 2023, had overtaken the university area. By the final year, the city centre accounted for roughly two fifths of all trips across the three locations, the largest share.

The figures are the same, but the report now gives a clear overview, groups the broadly stable series together and contrasts it with the rising trends. Trend language is precise, and the past tense is consistent because the graph covers a completed period. The final proportion is approximate and supported by the data: 45,000 out of 113,000 is roughly two fifths.

What changed between Band 6 and Band 8?

Six things changed:

  1. Selected the main story. The overview identifies the rising series and the stable one rather than saying only that the figures changed.
  2. Grouped related information. The city centre and riverside both rose; the university figure stayed close to its starting point.
  3. Compared the series. The report notes how the city centre moved from below the university area to the highest total.
  4. Chose accurate trend words. “Levelled off” describes a plateau; “rose steadily” describes the riverside’s consistent growth.
  5. Kept figures in proportion. Approximate descriptions are tied to the totals, not guessed.
  6. Controlled grammar and tense. Complex structures add relationships between ideas, while the time frame keeps verbs in the past.

In practice, task achievement is not about including every data point. Examiners assess whether you identify and clearly present the key features. Three myths about a Band 8 IELTS Task 1 answer

Myth: Band 8 answers are longer. They need to meet the 150-word minimum, but extra length is not the goal. A concise report with a clear overview and relevant details is more effective than a long list.

Myth: They are full of rare words. Precise, familiar language often works best. “Rose steadily” is more useful than an impressive-sounding verb that does not fit the trend.

Myth: Every sentence must be complex. Simple, accurate sentences can contribute to a high score when the report is logically grouped and makes meaningful comparisons.

Practise the upgrade

Warm-up: check your band guess

The opening excerpt was Band 6-style because it used vague language and missed the main comparisons. Revisit it and replace “increased a lot” with a more precise description.

Diagnosis: match each sentence to the main weakness

Match the examples from the Band 6 report to the criterion they most directly weaken.

Sentences

A. “Overall, the figures changed during the period and there were more trips at the end.”

B. “The number of trips in the city centre was 20,000 in 2019.”

C. “The figure went up to 25,000 … and then it rise to 40,000.”

D. “This is a lot more than before.”

E. “For riverside, the number was 10,000 … In 2022 it was 30,000.”

F. “The number of trips in the university area were 30,000 in 2019.”

Criteria: Task achievement and overview; coherence and cohesion; lexical resource; grammatical range and accuracy.

Answers:

A, Task achievement and overview: it does not identify a key trend.

B, Lexical resource: the repetitive sentence stem could be varied.

C, Grammatical range and accuracy: “rise” should be “rose”.

D, Lexical resource: “a lot more” is imprecise.

E, Coherence and cohesion: the detail is listed without a clear comparison or grouping.

F, Grammatical range and accuracy: “number” is singular, so use “was”.

Upgrade drill

Rewrite each sentence with more precise language or a clearer relationship between ideas.

  1. “The city centre went up a lot from 2020 to 2022.”
  2. “The university was 30,000 in 2019. It was 33,000 in 2021.”
  3. “Riverside had 10,000 trips in 2019 and 35,000 in 2023.”

Possible answers:

  1. “City-centre trips rose sharply from 25,000 in 2020 to 45,000 in 2022.”
  2. “The university figure edged up from 30,000 in 2019 to 33,000 in 2021.”
  3. “Riverside trips more than tripled, climbing from 10,000 in 2019 to 35,000 in 2023.”

Self-audit your next report

Before you finish a practice task, check:

  • Have I written an overview that identifies the main trends?
  • Have I grouped related series or periods?
  • Have I made at least one useful comparison?
  • Are my trend verbs and approximations accurate?
  • Do my tenses match the time period?
  • Have I included relevant figures without listing every point?

Turn the comparison into a repeatable method

The difference between these reports is not a secret vocabulary list. It is a repeatable process: spot the main trends, group related data, compare clearly and check that every detail is accurate.
