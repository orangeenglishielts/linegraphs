IELTS Line Graph Model Answer, Annotated by a Former Examiner

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 6 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

Line graphs are one of the most common charts in IELTS Academic Writing Task 1. When I was an IELTS examiner, they were also where I saw many candidates lose marks. The problem was rarely grammar. Usually, the description didn't match the data, or there was no clear overview.

Below is a practice task, a model answer written to a high standard, and my notes on what an examiner is looking for in each paragraph.

The task

The line graph shows the share of everyday purchases paid for by cash, card and mobile phone in one European country between 2010 and 2025. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.

You'll find the graph at the top of this post. The data is fictional and was created for practice.

Before you write: three minutes of planning

  • Read the axes. The figures are percentages of purchases, so every sentence must talk about shares or proportions, not numbers of people.
  • Find the big changes. Cash fell steeply, mobile payments took off, and card payments rose to a peak and then fell slightly.
  • Decide how to group the data. A time split works well here: 2010 to 2020 in one paragraph, and 2020 to 2025 in the next.

Exercise: pick out the main features

Look at the graph at the top of this post. Before you read the model answer, decide whether each point belongs in your overview.

Cash went from the most common way to pay to the least common.

Main feature. This is the biggest change, and it covers the whole period.

Card payments made up a quarter of purchases in 2010.

Minor detail. This is a useful figure for a body paragraph.

Mobile payments grew rapidly.

Main feature. It describes the overall trend for this method, with no figures.

Mobile payments stood at 2% in 2010.

Minor detail. Save exact starting points for the body paragraphs.

The model answer (161 words)

Show model answer

The line graph compares the proportion of everyday purchases in one European country that were paid for by cash, card and mobile phone between 2010 and 2025.
Overall, cash went from being by far the most common way to pay to the least common, while mobile payments grew rapidly. Card payments rose for most of the period, but fell slightly at the end.
In 2010, almost three quarters of purchases (73%) were made with cash, compared with a quarter by card and just 2% by mobile phone. Over the next decade, the use of cash fell sharply to 30%. Card payments overtook cash in around 2017 and reached a peak of 55% in 2020.
The most striking change in the final five years was in mobile payments, which more than doubled from 15% to 35%. By contrast, cash continued to decline, ending the period at only 15%, and card use dipped slightly to 50%, although it remained the most popular method.

Examiner notes, paragraph by paragraph

Introduction

The introduction paraphrases the task instead of copying it. "Shows the share" becomes "compares the proportion", while "cash, card and mobile phone" stays the same, because there's no natural alternative for those words. One sentence is enough.

Overview

This is the most important paragraph for Task Achievement. It picks out the main trend for each method, with no figures, so the examiner can see straight away that you've understood the graph. Without an overview, you can’t fully meet the Task Achievement criterion, which will hold your score back.

Body paragraph 1

The figures support each claim, and they're selected rather than listed: the starting points, the moment card overtakes cash, and the peak. Notice the variety in how numbers are given: "almost three quarters (73%)", "a quarter", "just 2%". Words like almost and just show that the writer is interpreting the data, not copying it.

"In around 2017" is an estimate, because the graph has no data point for that year. The word around makes that clear. Never invent precise figures between data points.

Body paragraph 2

The paragraph opens with the biggest change in the period, then contrasts it with the other two lines. The cohesion comes from meaning, not from a linking word at the start of every sentence: which connects the figures to mobile payments, By contrast signals the shift, and although it remained adds an important qualification.

The grammar is varied but controlled: a relative clause, a participle clause ("ending the period at only 15%") and a concession, all without errors.

What about a conclusion?

Task 1 doesn't need a conclusion. The overview already does that job, so use the time to check your figures instead.

How the answer meets the four criteria

  • Task Achievement: a clear overview, key features selected, accurate figures, and all three methods covered.
  • Coherence and Cohesion: logical paragraphs (overview, then two time periods) and a range of cohesive devices used naturally.
  • Lexical Resource: precise trend language such as fell sharply, overtook, reached a peak, more than doubled and dipped slightly, plus collocations like "by far the most common".
  • Grammatical Range and Accuracy: a mix of simple and complex sentences, active and passive forms, and comparisons, with no errors.

What a Band 6 answer often does instead

  • Leaves out the overview.
  • Lists every figure for every year, line by line.
  • Uses verbs that don't match the size of the change, such as "rose dramatically" for a rise of three percentage points.
  • Makes small but frequent errors, such as "increased of 20%" instead of "increased by 20%".

Now try it yourself

Cover the model answer and write your own in 20 minutes. Then compare. Did you write an overview? Is every figure accurate? Did you group the information, or list it?
