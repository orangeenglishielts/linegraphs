IELTS Line Graphs With Future Projections: Tenses and Phrases

By Lindsey, former IELTS examiner. 12 November 2026. Read this post with its interactive exercises.

How to describe future projections in an IELTS Task 1 line graph: which tenses to use, useful phrases and a Band 6 vs Band 7 example.

Some IELTS line graphs don't stop at the present. Part of the line turns into a dotted line, or the years carry on to 2030 or 2040. That part shows a projection: what someone expects to happen, not what has already happened.

As a former IELTS examiner, I saw two problems with these graphs again and again. Some candidates described the whole graph in the past tense, as if 2040 had already happened. Others noticed the change but used will in every sentence, which sounds repetitive and more certain than the data really is.

How to spot a projection in an IELTS line graph

  • Part of the line is dotted or dashed.
  • The years go beyond the present, or are labelled projected, forecast or estimated.
  • The title uses words like expected, predicted or projected.

Check the title and the key before you write anything. It takes ten seconds and tells you which tenses you'll need.

Which tenses to use for past and future data

  • Past data: past simple. Sales rose from 2 million in 2015 to 9 million in 2025.
  • Data that runs up to now: present perfect. Sales have risen steadily since 2015.
  • Future data: a forecast phrase. Sales are expected to reach 20 million by 2035.
  • A level reached by a future date: future perfect. By 2035, sales will have doubled.

Phrases for describing future projections

  • is expected / predicted / projected / forecast to + verb: Sales are projected to rise.
  • is set to + verb, for a clear, steep change: Sales are set to double.
  • according to the projections or the forecast suggests that: useful at the start of a sentence.
  • will + verb: fine now and then, but mix it with the phrases above.
  • Nouns: a projected rise, an expected fall, a forecast increase of 20%.

A practice line graph with projections

The graph below is made up for practice. It shows sales of electric cars in three countries from 2015 to 2035, in millions. The figures for 2030 and 2035 are projections, shown with dotted lines.

Here are the figures, in millions:

  • Country A: 1 (2015), 3 (2020), 6 (2025), 10 (2030, projected), 14 (2035, projected)
  • Country B: 2 (2015), 3 (2020), 4 (2025), 4.5 (2030, projected), 5 (2035, projected)
  • Country C: 0.5 (2015), 1 (2020), 2 (2025), 5 (2030, projected), 9 (2035, projected)

Band 6 vs Band 7: describing a projection

Here's the kind of sentence I often saw in answers around Band 6:

In 2030 Country A will be 10 and in 2035 it will be 14. Country C will be 5 and then 9.

It lists numbers, repeats will be, never says what is being measured, and doesn't compare anything.

Here's a version with the features a Band 7 answer needs:

Sales in Country A are expected to keep climbing, reaching 14 million by 2035, while Country C is projected to see the sharpest growth, with sales rising more than fourfold to 9 million.

It names the subject (sales), uses two different forecast phrases, picks out the key figures and makes a comparison.

How to write the overview

Your overview should cover both parts of the graph: what happened and what is expected.

Overall, electric car sales grew in all three countries and are expected to keep rising until 2035. Country A had become the largest market by 2025 and is projected to stay well ahead, while Country C is predicted to overtake Country B by 2030.

Practice: complete the sentences

Complete each sentence about the practice graph, using the verb in brackets.

  1. Between 2015 and 2025, sales in Country B ______ (double).
  2. By 2035, sales in Country A ______ (rise) to 14 million.
  3. Country C ______ (overtake) Country B by 2030.
  4. By 2035, sales in Country C ______ (increase) more than fourfold compared with 2025.
  5. In 2020, sales in Countries A and B ______ (be) equal, at 3 million each.

Answers: 1. doubled 2. are expected to rise (or are projected to rise) 3. is predicted to overtake 4. will have increased 5. were
